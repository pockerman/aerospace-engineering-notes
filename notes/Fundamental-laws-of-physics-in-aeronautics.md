# Fundamental laws of physics in aeronautics

## Overview

In this section we will review the fundamental laws of physics that govern aeronautics. In other words, we will look into

- Newton's laws of motion
- Bernoulli's principle

## Newton's laws of motion

Let's briefly recap Newton's laws of motion.

#### Newton's 1st law of motion

Newton's first law says the following:

_A body will remain at rest or in uniform motion unless an external force acts on the body_

In other words the motion state of the body will be disrupted if an external force acts on it. Newton's first law explains why we need
thrust in order to fly an aircraft. Thrust causes the airplane to accelerate.


#### Newton's 2nd law of motion


Newton's second law says the following:

_The force equals mass times acceleration_

Or in mathematical terms

$$F = m \times a$$

The acceleration is the derivative of the velocity i.e. $a = dv/dt$. Thus the second law tells us how much force we need in order to change the velocity
of the body. In terms of aeronautics, the engines of the aircraft must provide enough thrust to accelerate the mass of the airplane forward overcoming drag and gravity.


----
**Example 1**

How much constant force is needed to apply on an object at rest in order for the body to reach a velocity of $2 m/sec$ in $1 sec$ when the mass of the body is $2 kg$?

In this case the acceleration we need to achieve is $a = \Delta v / \Delta t = (2 m/sec) / (1 sec) = 2 m/sec^2$ and for a body of $2 kg$ Newton's second law gives us

$$ F= 2 kg \times 2 m/sec^2 = 4N$$

----


#### Newton's 3rd law of motion

Newton's third law says the following:

_For every action there is an equal and opposite reaction_.

The application of this law in aeronautics is mostly evident with jet engines whereby expelling exhaust gases backwards at high speeds and in turn the aircraft moves forward.


## Bernoulli's principle

In very simple terms, Bernoulli's principle states the following:

_As the velocity of a fluid increases, its pressure decreases_

----

## Summary

In this section we reviewed the fundamental laws of physics that govern aeronautics. In particular:

- Newton's 1st law: a body remains at rest or in uniform motion unless an external force acts on it. This is why an aircraft needs thrust in order to accelerate.
- Newton's 2nd law: $F = m \times a$. It tells us how much force is needed to change the velocity of a body; the engines must provide enough thrust to accelerate the aircraft's mass while overcoming drag and gravity.
- Newton's 3rd law: for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction. Jet engines expel exhaust gases backwards at high speed and, in turn, the aircraft moves forward.
- Bernoulli's principle: as the velocity of a fluid increases, its pressure decreases. This principle helps us understand how lift is generated.
